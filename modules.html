DomeForge Modules

Explore The Geometry Engines Behind DomeForge

DomeForge currently includes fifteen geometry engines and two visualization players organized into four broad categories: Classic Geometry, Patterned Geometry, Exotic Geometry, and Visualization Players. Expand any module below to learn about its geometric foundations, practical applications, and unique DomeForge capabilities.

Classic Geometry

Tetrahedron

Geometric Background

The Tetrahedron is the most basic of the Platonic solids. It has 4 vertices, 6 edges, and 4 equilateral triangle faces.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • Primarily used for educational exploration of geodesic and polyhedral geometry.
  • The default TetraDome truncated just below the equator works well in either vertex-down or vertex-up orientation for frequencies of 4 and above.
  • Taken to a maximum frequency of 16, the TetraDome reveals how this Platonic solid can evolve into a more architecturally interesting form that is more aesthetic than structurally practical.
Hexahedron / Cube

Geometric Background

The Cube is the most familiar of the Platonic solids. It has 8 vertices, 12 edges and 6 square faces.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • Primarily used for educational exploration of geodesic and polyhedral geometry.
  • A frequency 4 cube truncated at the equator produces a visually appealing yet simple dome with perfect equatorial truncation.
  • The X-Wise primary panel subdivision option demonstrates the cube's surprising architectural potential for dome structures.
Octahedron

Geometric Background

Many people visualize the Octahedron as two back-to-back square pyramids. It has 6 vertices, 12 edges, and 8 equilateral triangular faces. It is the second most popular base shape for geodesic dome builders.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • As a partial dome, the Octahedron lends itself well to joining flat walls and conventional structures.
  • DomeForge provides three subdivision methods, including an equal-primary option which generates equal chord lengths on the major panel boundaries.
  • A frequency 3 OctaDome creates an excellent beginner dome with only three strut lengths and two panel types.
  • A frequency 7 equal-primary OctaDome was used in the design of the end caps for the Belltown Dome in Ottawa, Canada.
Icosahedron

Geometric Background

The Icosahedron is the Platonic solid most frequently used in spherical subdivision. It has 12 vertices, 30 edges and 20 equilateral triangular faces. For many people the Icosahedron is synonymous with geodesic domes due to the work of Buckminster Fuller.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • Widely used for dome homes, exhibition structures and architectural domes.
  • The Equal Chords and Mexican (Equal Arcs 3GC) methods remain popular because they produce relatively small numbers of strut and panel families.
  • A frequency 3 Equal Chords IcosaDome requires only three unique strut lengths and two panel sizes.
  • The geometry behaves exceptionally well as frequencies increase.
  • DomeForge supports arbitrary truncation and adaptive band adjustment to create a perfectly flat base.
  • The truncation and adaptive controls encourage experimentation while searching for a target geometry.
  • Many famous domes trace their origins to Icosahedral geometry, including Spaceship Earth and the Montreal Biosphere.
Dodecahedron

Geometric Background

The Dodecahedron is the most complex Platonic solid with 12 pentagonal faces, 30 edges and 20 vertices. Its higher vertex count allows it to adapt naturally to spherical forms.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • The Dodecahedron is an underappreciated dome geometry because it does not truncate cleanly.
  • A simple frequency 2 DodecaDome truncated at -0.3 produces a practical dome with only 30 identical panels and two strut lengths.
  • Higher-frequency fullerene variants can create particularly striking architectural forms.

Patterned Geometry

Lamella

Geometric Overview

Lamella domes consist of horizontal rings combined with spiraling ribs. The resulting diamond and triangular patterns become progressively smaller toward the apex. Lamellas are known for repetitive parts, rapid fabrication, and visual elegance.

Practical Uses

  • Popular for large-span commercial structures, sports facilities, and architectural feature roofs.

DomeForge Features

  • Multiple Lamella variants.
  • User-selectable ring and rib frequencies.
  • Adjustable dome depth.
  • Squish factor for height compression.
Schwedler

Geometric Overview

The Schwedler Dome extends a simple ribbed dome by adding diagonal bracing members that create triangular panels. Its straightforward structure has made it popular for industrial and utility applications.

DomeForge Features

  • Multiple rib and bracing arrangements.
  • Optional Twist factor for Lamella-like visual effects.
  • Squish factor for reducing dome height.
Fibonacci Dome

Geometric Overview

Fibonacci Dome explores spherical structures based on phyllotaxis point distributions using the golden angle. Unlike Platonic subdivision, the geometry emerges from naturally distributed spiral point patterns.

DomeForge Features

  • Golden-angle Fibonacci point generation.
  • User-selectable point counts.
  • Dome truncation and real-world sizing controls.
  • Hybrid triangular and quadrilateral panelization.
ElliptiDome

Geometric Overview

ElliptiDome explores domes derived from elliptical rather than circular forms. This allows elongated domes and vault-like structures that better match many architectural requirements.

DomeForge Features

  • Preset ellipsoid families.
  • Support for forms reminiscent of the London Gherkin and geometric egg structures.
  • Elliptical frequency and truncation controls.
  • Extensive user-controlled variability.
WarpSurf

Geometric Overview

WarpSurf explores warped architectural surfaces built over rectangular or elliptical footprints. WarpSurf blends edge curvature, central dome shaping, and patterned panelization to create architecturally expressive roof and canopy forms. Examples range from gentle curved roofs to hyperbolic paraboloids reminiscent of the Calgary Saddledome.

DomeForge Features

  • Real-world envelope controls including width, depth and maximum height.
  • Edge curvature and twist controls.
  • Straight, Ellipse and Parabola profile options.
  • Multiple panelization patterns including rectangular, radial, Lamella, and Schwedler-inspired layouts.
  • Extensive user control supporting both practical and artistic structures.

Exotic Geometry

Snub Cube

Geometric Overview

The Snub Cube is one of the thirteen Archimedean solids. Its distinctive twisted symmetry produces one of the most visually dynamic appearances found in any regularly derived polyhedron.

DomeForge Exploration

  • Produces spherical panel patterns that appear to spiral around the structure.
  • Creates architectural forms that differ dramatically from conventional geodesic domes.
  • Ideal for artistic design studies and alternative dome concepts.
Snub Dodecahedron

Geometric Overview

The Snub Dodecahedron is an Archimedean solid derived through a chiral snub operation on the regular Dodecahedron. Its surface contains 80 triangles and 12 pentagons arranged in a beautifully swirling pattern. Pentagons are triangulated automatically by DomeForge.

DomeForge Exploration

  • Produces highly regular yet visually complex dome structures.
  • Well suited to advanced geometric exploration where symmetry and architectural impact are equally important.
Small Rhombicuboctahedron (SRCO)

Geometric Overview

The Small Rhombicuboctahedron contains 26 faces: 8 triangles and 18 squares. Each vertex joins one triangle and three squares to create a highly regular framework. DomeForge subdivides square faces into either two or four triangles prior to spherical projection.

DomeForge Exploration

  • Creates panel families that are straightforward to classify, fabricate and assemble.
  • Provides an excellent environment for exploring hybrid panel systems.
  • A useful alternative to purely triangular geodesic approaches.
Small Rhombicosidodecahedron (SRID)

Geometric Overview

The Small Rhombicosidodecahedron (SRID) contains 62 faces: 20 triangles, 30 squares and 12 pentagons. Each vertex joins one triangle, one square and one pentagon, producing a highly symmetric and evenly distributed framework. Squares and pentagons are triangulated by DomeForge.

DomeForge Exploration

  • Provides an elegant balance between geometric richness and structural regularity.
  • Excellent for hybrid panel systems and advanced structural studies.
  • Centroid-based pentagon subdivision and selectable quad subdivision produce remarkably uniform panel families.
  • Supports both low-frequency domes and higher-frequency geodesic refinements while maintaining excellent symmetry.
BiPyramid Dome (Johnson Solids #12, #13, #14 and #16)

Geometric Overview

Triangular and Pentagonal Bipyramids with regular faces are known as Johnson Solids #12 and #13. When elongated they become Johnson Solids #14 and #16. These composite polyhedra exhibit characteristics of both Platonic and Johnson geometries.

DomeForge Exploration

  • Interesting alternatives to conventional geodesic dome geometries.
  • The Triangular Bipyramid has similarities to the Tetrahedron.
  • The Pentagonal Bipyramid echoes some characteristics of Icosahedral geometry.
  • An excellent example of how combining regular solids can generate entirely new architectural possibilities.

Visualization Players

DomePlayer

Overview

DomePlayer is a simple visualization tool focused on the evaluation and comparison of lower-frequency dome geometries that builders may be considering for construction.

Applications

  • Load and compare candidate dome designs generated by the Platonic modules.
  • Focus on practical geometries with manageable strut schedules and panel families.
  • Share proposed structures with collaborators before committing to fabrication.
  • Quickly review geometry generated by DomeForge modules.
SpherePlayer

Overview

All DomeForge modules use color to illustrate panel and geometry families. As frequencies increase, the resulting geometric color patterns become increasingly interesting. These geometries can be collected into playlists and explored using SpherePlayer.

Features

  • Uses JSON files generated by DomeForge modules.
  • Explores large collections of domes and complete spheres.
  • Includes a sample playlist library representing many DomeForge geometries.
  • Provides build and dissolve animations.
  • Supports smooth continuous sphere rotation.
  • Functions as both a geometry viewer and a geometric screen saver.
