Classic Geometry

Tetrahedron

Geometric Background

The Tetrahedron is the most basic of the Platonic solids. It has 4 vertices, 6 edges, and 4 equilateral triangle faces.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • Primarily used for educational exploration of geodesic and polyhedral geometry.
  • The default TetraDome truncated just below the equator works well in either vertex down or vertex up orientation for frequencies of 4 and above.
  • Taken to a maximum 16 frequency our TetraDome reveals how this Platonic solid can evolve into a more architecturally interesting form that is more aesthetic than structurally practical/efficient.
Hexahedron / Cube

Geometric Background

This is the most familiar of the Platonic solids. It has 8 vertices, 12 edges and 6 square faces.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • Primarily used for educational exploration of geodesic and polyhedral geometry.
  • A 4 frequency truncated at the equator produces a visually appealing yet simple dome with perfect truncation at the equator.
  • We have also introduced the X-Wise primary panel subdivision option which shows off the Cube's architectural potential for domes.
Octahedron

Geometric Background

Many people visualize the Octahedron as 2 back-to-back square pyramids. It has 6 vertices, 12 edges, and 8 equilateral faces. It is the second most popular base shape for geodesic dome builders of both simple and higher frequency domes.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • As a partial dome, the Octahedron lends itself well to abut to flat walls/surfaces of other structures.
  • DomeForge provides 3 methods of Octahedron subdivision including an interesting version that generates a dome with equal chord lengths on the primary panel edges (great circles).
  • A 3 frequency OctaDome provides a perfect simple dome with only 3 strut lengths and 2 panel types good for backyard builders.
  • A 7 frequency equal-primary OctaDome was used to design the end caps of the Belltown Dome in Ottawa, Canada.
Icosahedron

Geometric Background

The icosahedron is the Platonic solid most frequently used in spherical polyhedron subdivision. It has 12 vertices, 30 edges and 20 equilateral faces, the most of any Platonic solid. The icosahedron is almost synonymous with Geodesic Domes and was famously popularized by Buckminster Fuller.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • At lower frequencies, the icosahedron is used for dome homes and recreational structures.
  • The Equal Chords and Mexican (Equal Arcs 3GC) subdivision methods are popular because they have low numbers of distinct strut lengths and panel sizes.
  • For example, a 3 frequency Equal Chords IcosaDome has only 3 unique strut lengths and 2 panel sizes making it straightforward to fabricate and assemble.
  • The icosahedron behaves well as frequencies increase and truncation levels vary.
  • DomeForge permits dome truncation at any level and provides an adaptive band tool to modify strut lengths and panel sizes near the truncation level to achieve a perfectly flat base.
  • The truncation level and adaptive tool are fun to use as you iterate toward a target geometry.
  • There are many spectacular higher-frequency IcosaDomes such as Spaceship Earth at EPCOT and the Biosphere in Montreal. You can get a good approximation of the Biosphere using a 12 frequency, -0.8 truncation and 0.02 adaptive band.
Dodecahedron

Geometric Background

The Dodecahedron is the most complex of the Platonic solids with 30 edges, 12 pentagonal faces and 20 vertices. Because of its complex geometry and high number of vertices it adapts naturally to the sphere.

Practical Uses / DomeForge Exploration

  • The dodecahedron seems like an under-utilized geodesic geometry for domes. This is likely because it does not truncate cleanly.
  • A simple DomeForge DodecaDome with a frequency of 2 and truncation at -0.3 actually produces a nice simple dome with 30 identical panels and 2 strut lengths.
  • At higher frequencies with the Fullerene option activated, some architecturally exciting fullerene domes and spheres can be created.

Patterned Geometry

Patterned Geometry

Lamella

Geometric Overview

Lamella domes have concentric rings in the horizontal plane with ribs that spiral up from the base. The rib pattern may be single or dual to create patterns of diamonds and/or triangles which get smaller towards the apex. Close to the apex a transition may occur to consolidate the number of struts and eliminate small panel sizes. Lamella domes have regular and repetitive strut and panel sizes which make manufacturing time fast and assembly easy.

Practical Uses

  • Lamella domes are popular for large shallow dome commercial applications such as sports facilities and for architectural applications within larger structures because of their natural beauty.

DomeForge Features

  • Multiple varieties of Lamellas are selectable.
  • Domes may be deep or shallow geometries with user selectable ring and rib frequency.
  • A "squish" factor may be applied to compress dome height.
Schwedler

Geometric Overview

The Schwedler Dome is the extension of a simple ribbed dome. It simply adds diagonal struts in a variety of orientations to create triangulated panels. Its regular and simple geometry make it popular for industrial applications such as tank covers. We have added a "Twist" option which adds more architectural flair to the basic rib layouts. Schwedler domes are easy to fabricate but not the most structurally efficient.

DomeForge Features

  • Multiple varieties of rib/bracing arrangements ranging from a simple rib/ring dome to a fully cross-braced version.
  • A "Twist" factor to skew vertical ribs in a Lamella-like way.
  • A "Squish" factor to compress the vertical height of the dome.
Fibonacci Dome

Geometric Overview

Fibonacci Dome explores spherical domes based on phyllotaxis point distributions using the golden angle. Points are laid out on the sphere in a spiral pattern, then truncated and panelized into a hybrid of triangular and quadrilateral panels. The result is a visually rich, highly regular dome that does not rely on Platonic subdivision.

DomeForge Features

  • Golden-angle Fibonacci point distribution with user-selectable point count.
  • Dome truncation and real-world diameter controls with units.
  • Stable nearest-neighbor strut network and hybrid triangle/quad panelization.
ElliptiDome

Geometric Overview

ElliptiDome explores domes and shells based on elliptical rather than circular shapes. This allows elongated domes and vault-like structures that better match real-world architectural constraints. Input parameters give users great flexibility to exercise their architectural and artistic creativity.

DomeForge Features

  • Preset Ellipsoid Types help begin exploration of forms such as the London "Gherkin" building or even a colorful geometric egg.
  • Frequency and truncation controls are adapted to elliptical layouts.
  • This module has more user-controlled variability than most others.
WarpSurf

Geometric Overview

WarpSurf explores warped surface geometries over rectangular or elliptical footprints. It blends edge curvature, central dome shaping, and patterned panelization to create architecturally expressive roof and canopy forms. For example, this module can produce a hyperbolic paraboloid that mimics the roof of the Calgary Saddledome.

DomeForge Features

  • Real-world envelope controls (width, depth, maximum height).
  • Edge curvature and twist controls with Straight, Ellipse, and Parabola profiles.
  • Multiple panelization patterns including rectangular, radial, lamella, and Schwedler-like arrangements.
  • The many user-controlled variables allow maximum user control and creativity.
